## Overview

The 27 November 2026 edition of the EDMO Weekly Pulse takes the collapse of COP31 — the UN climate summit held in Antalya, Turkey, in November 2026 — as its starting point for an editorial on how two decades of climate disinformation have made societies numb to climate failure. The edition argues that climate denial has mutated from outright rejection of the science into an attack on the alternatives (solar, wind, EVs, climate journalism and activists), precisely so that the cost of inaction appears lower than the cost of action. The ZOOM-IN section documents three climate-disinformation cases from the EDMO network (ADMO, GADMO, HDMO), including a joint Maldita.es–Science Feedback investigation showing that TikTok and YouTube moderation of climate disinformation is largely cosmetic. ELECTION BEAT covers Vietnamese "content farms" exploiting Spain's general election, an AI-generated image of allegedly missing PP/VOX ballot papers, and a French fact-check of Raphaël Glucksmann's wealth-inequality claim. GLOBAL PULSE covers Kremlin narratives resurfacing in Poland, a Sri Lankan network reviving a fake Christine Lagarde quote, and the "targeted individuals" online communities; ON A DIFFERENT NOTE features BROD/Funky Citizens' 11 SEPIA investigations into platform moderation in Romania.

## ON THE RISE — The COP31 failure, and how climate disinformation makes us numb

The collapse of COP31, the UN climate summit held in Antalya, Turkey, in November 2026, offers a stark snapshot of the new world order into which humanity is drifting. For the first time since the failed Copenhagen summit in 2009, a UN climate conference ended without an overarching agreement: the EU, the UK and other countries rejected conclusions that — because of pressure from major oil and gas producers like Saudi Arabia and Russia — did not mention fossil fuels. For the second year in a row the United States, the world's second-largest annual greenhouse-gas emitter and by far its largest historical contributor to cumulative CO₂ emissions, was absent; the Trump administration holds that climate policies are a threat to the economy and that regulation should be avoided. Meanwhile 2026 is likely to be the second-hottest year ever recorded, after the 2024 record, with 2025 likely to stay on the podium.

**How did we get here?** In 2018 Greta Thunberg's protest inspired Fridays for Future, a youth-led movement that brought millions onto the streets across Europe and beyond; the following year the World Economic Forum described climate change as the world's biggest threat, according to a Pew Research Center survey. Then the global agenda changed: the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and successive Middle East conflicts reshaped political priorities. Energy security, inflation, military spending and competitiveness came to dominate government agendas. Disruption of Russian gas supplies, instability around strategic routes such as the Strait of Hormuz and rising energy costs reinforced calls to prioritise short-term economic and industrial interests, and climate policy was increasingly framed as a burden rather than an investment.

This political retreat would have been much harder to sell had the information space not been compromised by the strategic dissemination of disinformation. For years countless demonstrably false stories about climate change — spread for political calculus or economic interest — shaped public understanding of the crisis, making the choice of immediate relief at the expense of a less dramatic future easier.

Until recently climate change itself, or its human origins, was simply denied. More recently, probably because the evidence from reality started piling up, climate disinformation shifted to attacking the alternatives: why accept sacrifices to reduce oil, gas or coal when solar panels "fry" and "kill all the animals"? When wind farms produce tonnes of polluting microplastics, make whales go crazy, kill dolphins and make reindeer infertile? Why put traditional carmakers under pressure if electric vehicles pollute just as much, stop working in cold weather and explode? Why trust traditional media on climate impacts, when they are spreading baseless panic? And why trust activists like Thunberg, when they are "stupid and privileged hypocrites"?

Hard choices arise when weighing two painful options; climate disinformation seeks to tip the balance by making one appear less costly — easier still when the other side of the scales is burdened by war and economic hardship. Even the failure of COP31 is unlikely to deliver a shock strong enough to reverse the balance. What might shift it again is more frequent extreme weather and natural disasters — hardly good news for humanity.

## ZOOM-IN — A Closer Look at Cases Detected by the EDMO Network

Climate disinformation spikes during heatwaves and disasters while dripping continuously across seasons and geographies with old and new tropes against climate action. Three articles examine the latest instances detected by the EDMO network — false political statements on alleged harmful effects of solar panels, recycled outdated studies on infrasound from wind turbines — and persistent shortcomings in platforms' content moderation.

### Trump falsely claims solar panels "kill all the animals" (ADMO)

While campaigning for Republicans in Lubbock, Texas, US President Donald Trump announced a sweeping rollback of rules curbing methane emissions from oil and gas producers, repeating his distaste for renewables; about solar panels he claimed they "fry" and "kill all the animals". Experts interviewed by AFP deny this. Solar panels may reach 55–70 °C on very hot days, but animals briefly touching or living near them are not burned; studies show panels typically cool the space underneath — where most critters live — and provide shade for species such as sheep. The article distinguishes photovoltaic panels from concentrated solar power (CSP), whose thousands of mirrors can indeed harm birds, although CSP is a minority technology and avian mortality is far lower than from other human activities. As an expert told AFP, "less CO₂ in the atmosphere means less potential for climate change and thus a reduced chance for all living creatures — including humans — of being 'fried' by increased global temperatures".

### A video presents an outdated study on infrasound from wind turbines (GADMO)

A video circulating on social media features excerpts of a programme by German broadcaster SWR on a study suggesting infrasound from wind turbines could cause heart problems in nearby residents; the clip carries the "SWR Aktuell" logo and traces back to a 2018 SWR Facebook video. DPA recalls that researchers then stressed the need for further testing; in a more detailed study three years later they found heart-muscle contractility decreased measurably at very high infrasound levels of 100 dB or more — a study criticised for testing isolated heart-muscle tissue in a lab rather than exposure to actual turbines. More recent international studies find no evidence of health risks to residents from wind-turbine infrasound. SWR has distanced itself from the earlier reporting and removed the video from its media library in early 2023 — yet it still circulates.

### TikTok and YouTube's "actions" against climate disinformation are window dressing (HDMO)

A joint investigation by Spain's Maldita.es and France's Science Feedback highlights serious shortcomings in how TikTok and YouTube counter climate disinformation despite their own terms. From October 2025 to February 2026 researchers analysed more than 1,200 French- and Spanish-language videos, identifying 169 pieces promoting weather manipulation and chemtrail conspiracy theories and baseless allegations against climate science, in violation of platform policies. After notification, YouTube demonetised only 5% of the 82 notified videos without explanation; TikTok limited access to 20% of the 87 notified videos, deemed "moderately dangerous" conspiracies. None of the 147 appeals lodged by researchers led to a review. The investigation denounces ineffective, opaque and likely automated moderation, demanding more transparency, human review and coherent application of the platforms' own policies.

## ELECTION BEAT — Tracking electoral disinformation through EDMO Hubs

### Vietnamese Facebook "content farms" exploit Spain's election narratives for profit (IBERIFIER)

A Maldita.es investigation found a network of Facebook pages, most based in Vietnam, spreading misleading AI-generated content about Spain's general elections. These "content farms" mass-produce sensationalist content to increase engagement and redirect users to external sites where operators earn advertising revenue. The pages post stories about politicians, candidates and members of the Spanish royal family using manipulated images and emotional language; the investigation analysed 30 pages, most created in 2026 and several after the election was announced. Their main objective appears economic rather than political, though experts note political content is increasing because it generates outrage, traffic and revenue.

### Image of allegedly missing PP and VOX ballot papers is not real (IBERIFIER)

INFOVERITAS debunked a viral image showing electoral documents mailed to a voter in Cyprus without ballot papers for Partido Popular and VOX, shared with claims that parties had been excluded from overseas ballots, raising concerns about electoral integrity among Spaniards abroad. Analysis found typographical errors, blurry text and deviations from the official Spanish ballot layout; OpenAI's Verify tool indicated the image was AI-generated and did not depict real documentation.

### Has the wealth of France's richest people doubled in 10 years, as Raphaël Glucksmann claims? (DE FACTO)

During a televised debate on France's 2027 presidential election, Raphaël Glucksmann, the candidate selected by the left-wing primary, argued for higher inheritance taxation, claiming the 500 largest French fortunes doubled their wealth in ten years and linking wealth concentration to poverty and inequality. Fact-checking finds the statement broadly accurate — the richest owners more than doubled their fortunes. Based on the annual Challenges ranking, their wealth rose from €460 billion in 2015 to €1.128 trillion in 2025.

## GLOBAL PULSE — Disinformation narratives shaping the world's conversations

### Kremlin narratives on Ukraine resurface in Polish public debate (CEDMO)

Who is responsible for the war in Ukraine, and who is blocking peace? A CEDMO/Demagog fact-check examines claims that Ukraine sought to expand the conflict with Russia and bears responsibility for failed peace efforts. It finds that these arguments, promoted by Polish MEP Ewa Zajączkowska-Hernik, closely reflect recurring Kremlin narratives shifting blame for Russia's aggression onto Ukraine and its allies, and contrasts them with the historical record of Russia's actions since 2014.

### How a Sri Lankan network breathes new life into a fake Christine Lagarde quote (DE FACTO)

Les Observateurs traces the latest spread of a fabricated quote claiming Christine Lagarde said older people "live too long" and threaten the global economy. The quote has circulated since 2012 and appears to distort an IMF report on the financial implications of ageing populations rather than any Lagarde statement. The falsehood recently gained millions of views after amplification by a network of Sri Lanka-based Facebook pages linked to monetised political clickbait and known for promoting far-right content across multiple countries.

### Inside the online communities of "targeted individuals" (EDMO / Facta)

A Facta investigation explores growing online communities of self-described "targeted individuals" who believe they are monitored, harassed or controlled through secret technologies and "neural weapons". Drawing on interviews, expert analysis and international research, it examines how forums, Facebook groups and social media communities reinforce these beliefs, creating echo chambers that deepen paranoia, discourage professional support and in some cases contribute to radicalisation. Particularly visible in the United States, similar networks have also developed in Italy.

## Türkiye Bağlamı

Bu sayının çıkış noktası doğrudan Türkiye'yi ilgilendiriyor: COP31, Kasım 2026'da Antalya'da yapıldı ve 2009 Kopenhag'dan bu yana ilk kez kapsayıcı bir anlaşma olmadan kapandı; fosil yakızlardan söz etmeyen sonuç metnini AB, İngiltere ve diğer ülkeler reddetti. Türkiye, ev sahibi olarak "iklim diplomasisi merkezi" imajını güçlendirmeyi hedeflerken, zirvenin başarısızlığı ülke içindeki iklim iletişimi açısından da bir kırılma noktası: EDMO'nun işaret ettiği "çözümleri hedef alma" stratejisi (GES'in hayvanları "kızarttığı", rüzgâr türbinlerinin sağlık tehdidi olduğu iddiaları) Türkiye'deki yerel çevre ve enerji tartışmalarında da dolaşımda. TÜBİTAK ve üniversite iklim araştırmaları ile MEB müfredatındaki iklim okuryazarlığı içeriği, bu anlatılara karşı en somut savunma hatları olarak öne çıkıyor.
