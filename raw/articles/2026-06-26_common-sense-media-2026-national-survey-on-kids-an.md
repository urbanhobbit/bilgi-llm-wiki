# Common Sense Media: 2026 National Survey on Kids and Technology — Longitudinal Survey Methodology

**Source:** [https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/2026-national-survey-kids-technology](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/2026-national-survey-kids-technology)

## Summary

Common Sense Media's 2026 National Survey on Kids and Technology employs sophisticated and well-established survey research methodology to comprehensively capture the evolving landscape of children's digital technology use across American households. The research utilizes a large-scale, nationally representative probability-based sample frame with careful stratification across multiple demographic dimensions including age (ranging from early childhood through adolescence), gender identity, race and ethnicity categories, household income distribution, and geographic region and urbanicity. The survey instrument employs previously validated measurement scales for a comprehensive range of dimensions including technology access patterns across devices and connectivity, daily usage duration estimates across device types including smartphones, tablets, computers, and gaming consoles, content consumption preferences and genre patterns across platforms, online social interaction patterns including platform-specific engagement, and digital skill development including information literacy, content creation, and safety practices. Methodologically, the survey design addresses the significant challenge of measuring dynamic and rapidly evolving technology use patterns through the complementary use of detailed time-use diaries capturing granular activity sequences and categorical frequency questions providing broader usage estimates. The analytical framework includes comprehensive descriptive population estimates with appropriately calculated confidence intervals accounting for complex survey design effects, rigorous subgroup comparisons using survey-weighted regression models that properly account for sampling design, and systematic trend analysis comparing current findings with previous biennial survey waves to identify trajectories of change. Key methodological strengths that distinguish this research include the large sample size enabling sufficiently powered subgroup estimation for policy-relevant population segments, the consistent use of validated measurement scales across survey waves enabling reliable trend detection, and the transparent reporting of essential methodological parameters including survey design effects, margins of error calculated at standard confidence levels, and detailed response rate information. The survey demonstrates methodological foresight by addressing emergent measurement challenges including valid assessment of AI tool usage among children, reliable quantification of short-form video consumption patterns on rapidly evolving platforms, and comprehensive capture of cross-platform behavior patterns that characterize contemporary digital media engagement.

## Key Points

- Large-scale nationally representative probability-based survey design enables statistically precise population estimation of children's technology use patterns
- Complementary time-use diaries and categorical frequency questions address the measurement challenge of dynamic, rapidly evolving technology engagement behaviors
- Survey-weighted regression models accounting for complex survey design effects enable rigorous subgroup comparison across demographic categories
- Consistent longitudinal trend analysis across biennial survey waves enables reliable tracking of the rapidly evolving digital media landscape
- Proactive attention to emergent methodological challenges including AI tool measurement, short-form video quantification, and cross-platform behavior capture demonstrates methodological leadership
