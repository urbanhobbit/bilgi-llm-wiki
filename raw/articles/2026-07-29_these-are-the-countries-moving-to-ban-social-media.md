# These are the countries moving to ban social media for children | TechCrunch

**Source:** [https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/15/social-media-ban-children-countries-list/](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/15/social-media-ban-children-countries-list/)

## Summary

TechCrunch reporter Aisha Malik published this comprehensive roundup on June 15, 2026, covering the growing global movement to restrict children's access to social media platforms. Australia became the first country to implement such measures at the end of 2025, banning social media access for children under 16 with significant penalties for non-compliant platforms. The UK became the latest country to announce a ban on social media for children, with Prime Minister Keir Starmer making the landmark announcement on June 15, 2026. The article catalogs the various approaches being taken by governments worldwide, noting that these regulations aim to reduce risks including cyberbullying, addiction, mental health deterioration, and exposure to harmful or exploitative content. Some countries have implemented outright bans while others have taken more graduated approaches involving parental consent requirements, age verification mandates, or platform design obligations. The article examines differing enforcement mechanisms across jurisdictions, with some countries imposing substantial fines on platforms that fail to comply with child protection requirements. It also discusses the ongoing debate about effectiveness, the privacy implications of mandatory age verification systems, and concerns that over-restrictive measures might infringe on young people's rights to access information and participate in digital civic life. The piece highlights how child social media regulation has become one of the most significant and rapidly evolving technology policy debates globally in 2026.

## Key Points

- Australia first with under-16 social media ban (late 2025)
- UK PM Starmer announced ban June 15, 2026
- Multiple countries taking varied approaches to child social media restriction
- Targeted issues: cyberbullying, addiction, mental health, harmful content
- Enforcement ranges from fines to parental consent requirements
- Debate on privacy implications of age verification systems
