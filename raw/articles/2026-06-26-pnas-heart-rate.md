# Heart rate synchrony as a marker of real-world social engagement

**Authors:** Hanlu He, J. Christensen, A. Sørensen, Ivana Konvalinka, Stephen M. Fleming
**Published:** 25 December 2025
**Venue:** PNAS Nexus
**DOI:** 10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag181

## Abstract

Human social behaviour unfolds in complex real-world environments influenced by social and environmental factors, yet reliable markers of social engagement and connection remain elusive. Interpersonal physiological synchrony (IPS) has been proposed as one such marker. This study continuously measured heart rate, GPS, and the acoustic environment from 72 participants across three multi-day trips to New York City. Heart rates reliably synchronized when participants were in close physical proximity. IPS was stronger among socially familiar peers, and context dependent, emerging during close-proximity interactions and joint attention to shared stimuli. Acoustic context modulated IPS — low-to-moderate sound pressure levels enhanced synchrony, while excessive environmental noise reduced it.

## Key Findings

1. Physical proximity → reliable heart rate synchrony
2. Social familiarity strengthens physiological synchrony
3. Joint attention (shared stimuli) triggers synchrony
4. Environmental noise degrades synchrony

## Significance for Children & Digital World

- Digital vs. face-to-face socialisation: physiological synchrony requires physical presence
- Screen time debates: do digital interactions provide the same social bonding?
- Turkey-specific: Gençler günde 4.2 saat SM'de, yüz yüze etkileşim azalıyor
- Implications for school environment design (acoustic context matters)
