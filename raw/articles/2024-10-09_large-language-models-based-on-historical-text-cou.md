# Large Language Models based on historical text could offer informative tools for behavioral science

**Author(s):** Michael E. W. Varnum, Nicolas Baumard, Mohammad Atari, Kurt Gray

**Published:** 2024-10-09

**Source:** [https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2407639121](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2407639121)

## Summary

This article explores the potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) trained on historical texts as novel tools for behavioral science research. By leveraging LLMs' ability to process and analyze vast corpora of historical texts including books, newspapers, and other written records spanning centuries, researchers can gain new insights into how human behavior, social norms, values, and psychological characteristics have evolved over time. The paper discusses how these models can be used to test hypotheses about historical psychological change, track cultural evolution, and identify long-term behavioral patterns that would be impossible to study through traditional empirical methods. The authors argue that LLMs offer a promising complement to existing methods in historical psychology, cultural evolution, and computational social science, while also addressing limitations including concerns about historical bias in source texts, model interpretability, and the need for validation against established historical and behavioral data.

## Key Points

- LLMs trained on historical texts can help study long-term behavioral and psychological change
- Enables testing of hypotheses about cultural evolution and historical psychology at scale
- Potential to track changing social norms, values, and emotional expression over centuries
- Complementary to traditional methods in computational social science and behavioral science
- Limitations include historical source biases, model interpretability, and validation challenges
