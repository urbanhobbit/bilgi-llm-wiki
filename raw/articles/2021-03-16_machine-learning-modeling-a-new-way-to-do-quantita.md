# Machine Learning Modeling: A New Way to do Quantitative Research in Social Sciences in the Era of AI

**Author(s):** Jiaxing Zhang, Shuaishuai Feng

**Published:** 2021-03-16

**Source:** [https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/JWE/article/view/5903](https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/JWE/article/view/5903)

## Summary

Machine learning modeling offers a new paradigm for quantitative research in the social sciences. Traditional quantitative models rely heavily on theoretical hypotheses and statistics but fail to acknowledge the problem of overfitting, causing research results to be less generalizable and leading to social predictions being ignored. Machine learning models that use cross validation and regularization can effectively solve the problem of overfitting, providing support for prediction-based social science research. This paper discusses the sources and internal mechanisms of overfitting, introduces machine learning modeling through its high-level ideas, goals, and concrete methods, and examines the shortcomings and limiting factors of machine learning models. The authors argue that using machine learning in social sciences research is an opportunity and not a threat.

## Key Points

- Overfitting is a critical but often ignored problem in traditional social science quantitative research
- Cross-validation and regularization are key ML techniques that address overfitting
- ML enables a shift from explanation-oriented to prediction-oriented social science research
- The paper covers Lasso regression, cross-validation, and model selection methods
- Authors advocate combining traditional statistical methods with ML approaches
