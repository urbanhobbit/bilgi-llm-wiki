# Kids Over Clicks — Project 2029 Comprehensive Online Safety Agenda

**Author(s):** Project 2029

**Published:** 2026-06-29

**Source:** [https://www.project2029foramerica.org/post/kids-over-clicks](https://www.project2029foramerica.org/post/kids-over-clicks)

## Summary

Project 2029's 'Kids Over Clicks' proposal is a comprehensive online safety policy agenda framed as 'keeping kids as safe online as we do on the road or at home'. The document outlines the problem through extensive research: teens spend 5+ hours daily on social media, 1 in 3 kids shows addictive social media use patterns by end of middle school, linked to higher rates of anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideation. Specific policy proposals include: (1) banning social media accounts for kids under 16, (2) holding tech companies accountable by narrowing Section 230 protections for AI-generated content, paid ads, and illegal content, (3) banning cell phones in schools to improve learning outcomes, (4) promoting a smartphone-free childhood until age 14 through executive and cultural action, (5) adopting stronger default privacy protections including banning surveillance advertising and limiting children's data collection, and (6) designing safer digital products through mandated safety standards. The proposal is endorsed by Jonathan Haidt, Sen. Cory Booker, Gov. Mikie Sherrill, and AFT President Randi Weingarten.

## Key Points

- Social media ban for under-16 as centerpiece proposal
- Section 230 reform to hold platforms liable for AI content, ads, and illegal material
- Cell phone bans in schools with exception frameworks
- Smartphone-free childhood campaign until age 14 via executive action
- Ban on surveillance advertising and children's data collection limits
- Mandated safety standards for digital products (product safety model)
- Designed to be enacted partly through executive action without Congress
- Calls social media this generation's 'tobacco moment'
