## Summary

Politico EU reports on the Irish government's decision to cut motor fuel taxes in a bid to address rising public discontent and protests over the cost of living. The article examines the policy trade-offs facing governments across Europe as they balance fiscal responsibility with political pressures from inflation-weary voters. Ireland's fuel tax cut is analyzed in the context of broader European debates about green taxation, the just transition to carbon neutrality, and the political sustainability of carbon pricing mechanisms. The article explores how cost-of-living pressures are forcing governments to recalibrate their climate policies, potentially slowing the pace of the green transition.
