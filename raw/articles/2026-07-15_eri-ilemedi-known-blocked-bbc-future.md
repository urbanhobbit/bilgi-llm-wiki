## Summary

This BBC Future article, originally published around March 2026, explores how children are actually engaging with artificial intelligence tools in their daily lives, moving beyond the sensationalized headlines to understand the real patterns of usage, benefits, and risks. The piece is part of BBC's ongoing coverage of technology's impact on childhood and examines the gap between adult fears about AI and children's practical experiences with the technology. Based on interviews with educators, child development experts, and children themselves, the article investigates AI tools such as ChatGPT, image generators, voice assistants, and personalized learning platforms that have become increasingly embedded in children's educational and recreational activities. The article addresses key questions including whether children understand that AI can produce inaccurate or biased information, how they navigate the boundaries between AI-assisted learning and over-reliance on automated tools, what safeguards families and schools need to implement, and how parents can effectively monitor and guide their children's AI interactions. It also discusses the regulatory implications as governments worldwide grapple with the rapid integration of AI into children's digital ecosystems, including the challenge of creating age-appropriate AI design standards that protect children without stifling innovation. The BBC's coverage typically balances the potential educational benefits of AI with necessary caution about data privacy, developmental appropriateness, and the need for robust child protection frameworks in the age of generative AI.
