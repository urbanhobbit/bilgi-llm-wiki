# Common Sense Media: State of Kids and Families in America 2026 — Survey Research Methodology

**Source:** [https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-state-of-kids-and-families-in-america-2026](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-state-of-kids-and-families-in-america-2026)

## Summary

Common Sense Media's 'The State of Kids and Families in America 2026' report employs rigorous survey research methodology to comprehensively capture the evolving landscape of children's digital media engagement within contemporary family contexts. The research utilizes a nationally representative sampling framework employing stratified random sampling techniques to ensure robust demographic diversity across socioeconomic status, geographic region, race and ethnicity categories, family structure types, and urban-suburban-rural classifications. The survey instrument employs validated and field-tested measurement scales for multiple dimensions including media usage intensity and patterns, parental mediation and co-use strategies, digital wellness and screen balance indicators, family technology dynamics and household digital ecology, and digital equity dimensions including device access and broadband connectivity. Methodologically, the report directly addresses key challenges in family media research including social desirability bias affecting self-reported media use estimates, recall accuracy limitations in time-use estimation over extended recall periods, and the increasing complexity of measuring concurrent media multitasking across multiple devices and platforms. The analytical framework employs comprehensive descriptive statistics, rigorous subgroup comparisons using chi-square tests and t-tests with appropriate multiple comparison corrections, and multivariate regression models systematically controlling for potential demographic and socioeconomic covariates. Longitudinal trend analysis embedded in the research design enables robust tracking of changes in media use patterns across successive biennial survey waves, providing essential data on the trajectory of children's digital media engagement. The methodology section demonstrates best practices by transparently reporting response rates, sampling error margins at standard confidence levels, and honest acknowledgment of study limitations including the exclusion of non-English-speaking households and the inherent challenges of measuring engagement with rapidly evolving digital platforms and services.

## Key Points

- Nationally representative stratified probability sampling ensures demographic diversity and generalizability in family digital media research
- Validated multi-dimensional measurement scales capture media usage, parental mediation, digital wellness, and family technology dynamics comprehensively
- Social desirability bias, recall accuracy limitations, and multi-platform measurement complexity present key methodological challenges addressed through careful design
- Multivariate regression modeling with comprehensive demographic covariates enables robust analysis of technology-family relationships
- Longitudinal trend analysis across successive biennial survey waves enables reliable tracking of changes in family digital media ecology over time

Türkiye'de bu alanda önde gelen araştırma merkezleri arasında İstanbul Üniversitesi İletişim Fakültesi Dijital Medya Araştırmaları Merkezi, Ankara Üniversitesi İletişim Araştırmaları Merkezi (İLAM), Marmara Üniversitesi İletişim Fakültesi Medya ve Dijital Çalışmalar Merkezi ve Üsküdar Üniversitesi Dijital Medya ve Çocuk Araştırmaları Merkezi sayılabilir.
