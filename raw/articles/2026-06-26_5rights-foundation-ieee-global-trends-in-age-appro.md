# 5Rights Foundation / IEEE: Global Trends in Age-Appropriate Digital Design — Interdisciplinary Research Methodology

**Source:** [https://5rightsfoundation.com/ieee-analysis-suggests-growing-global-trend-toward-age-appropriate-design](https://5rightsfoundation.com/ieee-analysis-suggests-growing-global-trend-toward-age-appropriate-design)

## Summary

The 5Rights Foundation's analytical report on IEEE research examining growing global trends toward age-appropriate digital design constitutes a significant methodological contribution to the interdisciplinary field of children's digital rights research. The analysis systematically synthesizes IEEE's technical standards development methodology, which involves rigorous consensus-building processes among technical experts, with comprehensive children's rights frameworks grounded in international law. Methodologically, the research employs comparative legal analysis across multiple regulatory jurisdictions including the UK Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code), the EU Digital Services Act child safety provisions and its associated codes of conduct, the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) reform efforts including the Kids Online Safety Act, and the Australian Online Safety Act and its Basic Online Safety Expectations determination. This comparative methodology is combined with detailed technical standards analysis examining specifications developed by IEEE, ISO, and IETF working groups, and multi-stakeholder consultation processes capturing perspectives from industry, civil society, academia, and child rights organizations. The research framework innovatively integrates rights-based impact assessment methodology grounded in the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child General Comment No. 25, which provides authoritative guidance on children's rights in digital environments, with rigorous technical feasibility evaluation examining implementation constraints and practical deployment considerations. The systematic review methodology comprehensively surveys age assurance and verification technologies including various technical approaches, privacy-by-design implementation patterns incorporating data minimization and purpose limitation principles, content moderation systems specifically designed for child users with age-appropriate sensitivity, and safety-by-default interface design practices. This analysis provides a methodological template for conducting rigorous interdisciplinary research at the critical intersection of children's rights, digital regulation, and technical standards development, demonstrating how normative rights frameworks can be productively operationalized through technical standards scholarship.

## Key Points

- Interdisciplinary methodology integrates IEEE technical standards analysis with international children's rights frameworks grounded in the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child
- Comparative legal analysis across UK, EU, US, and Australian jurisdictions reveals converging global regulatory trends in age-appropriate design requirements
- UN Convention on the Rights of the Child General Comment No. 25 provides an authoritative analytical framework for evaluating children's digital environments
- Age assurance technologies, privacy-by-design implementation, and child-specific content moderation systems are key technical domains requiring specialized methodology
- Rights-based impact assessment methodology combined with technical feasibility evaluation enables effective regulatory design balancing protection and participation rights
