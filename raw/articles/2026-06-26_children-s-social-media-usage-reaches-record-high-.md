# Children's Social Media Usage Reaches Record High Globally — The Guardian Analysis

**Source:** [https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/feb/28/children-social-media-usage-record](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/feb/28/children-social-media-usage-record)

## Summary

The Guardian's comprehensive coverage examines the complex and multifaceted relationship between children and social media platforms, analyzing both the opportunities for connection, creativity, and learning that digital platforms offer young people and the serious concerns about addiction, mental health impacts, exposure to harmful content, data exploitation, and erosion of privacy. The reporting spans scientific research documenting correlations between heavy social media use and increased rates of anxiety, depression, and sleep disorders among adolescents, as well as the positive dimensions of online communities that provide support for marginalized youth. The Guardian has extensively covered the legislative landscape including the UK Online Safety Act's duty of care framework, the EU Digital Services Act's systemic risk assessment requirements, the California Age-Appropriate Design Code Act, and the wave of social media age restriction bills being considered in countries including France, Greece, Australia, and multiple US states. The coverage also examines the technical challenges of age verification, the tensions between privacy and safety in platform design, the commercial interests that drive engagement-optimized algorithms, and the perspectives of young people themselves on what they want from digital spaces. The reporting consistently emphasizes that effective policy must be evidence-based, human rights-respecting, and designed with meaningful input from children and young people.

## Key Points

- Complex relationship between children and social media involves both benefits and serious risks
- Strong evidence linking heavy use to anxiety, depression, and sleep disorders
- Growing legislative momentum across multiple countries for age-based access restrictions
- Effective policy must be evidence-based, rights-respecting, and informed by children's perspectives
