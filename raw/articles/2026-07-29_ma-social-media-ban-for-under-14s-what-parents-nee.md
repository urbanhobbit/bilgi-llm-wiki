## Summary

This article by Dr. David Park on the WhitelistVideo blog, updated May 19, 2026, provides a comprehensive analysis of Massachusetts' landmark legislation that bans social media for children under 14 and requires parental permission for 14 and 15-year-olds. The law, recognized as one of the toughest in the United States, represents a significant precedent for state-level child online safety regulation and is being closely watched by other states and countries considering similar measures. The article explains the law's key provisions in detail including a complete prohibition on social media accounts for children under 14, mandatory and verifiable parental consent mechanisms for teenagers aged 14 to 15, specific enforcement requirements for online platforms including robust age verification systems, data protection standards for minors' information, and graduated penalties for non-compliance ranging from warning notices to substantial fines. The implementation deadline is October 2026, which the article notes presents significant technical and operational challenges for technology companies that must build, test, and deploy age assurance systems. The piece offers practical guidance for Massachusetts parents on preparing for the law's implementation including understanding which platforms and services are affected, how parental consent processes will work in practice, what privacy considerations arise from age verification, and what additional steps families can take to ensure comprehensive online safety. The article also contextualizes Massachusetts' pioneering approach within the broader US landscape of state-level initiatives.

## Key Points

- Massachusetts bans social media for under-14s, requires parental consent for 14-15
- One of the toughest state-level laws in the US
- Implementation deadline: October 2026
- Requires platforms to implement age verification systems
- 16+ states considering similar legislation via Kids Code Coalition
- Practical parent guidance on preparing for new requirements
