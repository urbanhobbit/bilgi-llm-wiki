# Hungary Election 2026: TISZA Wins Landslide Victory - Euractiv

**Source:** [https://www.euractiv.com/section/europe/politics/hungary-election-tisza-wins-444](https://www.euractiv.com/section/europe/politics/hungary-election-tisza-wins-444)

## Summary

Euractiv reports comprehensively on the 2026 Hungarian parliamentary elections, described as a political earthquake in Central Europe following the TISZA party's landslide victory and the historic collapse of Viktor Orbán's Fidesz party after 14 years in power. The article provides detailed analysis of the election results from a distinctly Brussels-oriented perspective, emphasizing the implications for European Union governance, rule of law debates, and the balance of power within European institutions. Euractiv's coverage examines the vote breakdown across Hungary's constituencies, the demographic shifts that propelled TISZA to power — particularly the overwhelming support from young and urban voters disillusioned with corruption and democratic backsliding — and the economic grievances that eroded Fidesz's traditional rural base. The article explores the campaign dynamics, including the role of independent media and civil society organizations in challenging government propaganda, the impact of EU-related issues such as frozen cohesion funds on voter sentiment, and the effectiveness of TISZA's anti-corruption messaging. From a European policy perspective, the piece examines how the change in Hungarian leadership could unlock billions in frozen EU funds, shift the balance on European Council votes regarding Ukraine support and enlargement, and potentially reduce the influence of illiberal democratic models within the EU. The article also assesses the readiness of the incoming TISZA government to manage the transition, including plans to reform Hungary's judiciary, media regulatory bodies, and anti-corruption institutions, all of which will be closely scrutinized by the European Commission.

## Key Points

- Historic Hungarian election result - TISZA landslide
- End of Orbán era after 14 years
- Major implications for EU politics and rule of law
- Central European political realignment
- Turkey-Hungary relations may shift under new government
