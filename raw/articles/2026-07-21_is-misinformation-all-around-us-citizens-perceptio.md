# Is Misinformation All Around Us? Citizens’ Perceptions of Misinformation Nearness Regarding Its Span, Availability, and Accessibility

**Author(s):** T. G. van der Meer, M. Hameleers

**Published:** 2026

**Source:** [https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21670811.2026.2700585](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21670811.2026.2700585)

## Summary

This study investigates to what extent and how people perceive misinformation as a proximate phenomenon that has infiltrated their everyday digital news domains. We argue that the perception that misinformation is all around them is grounded in subjective assessments of nearness rather than objective measures of exposure, underscoring the importance of studying the perceptual and subjective dimensions of misinformation. Via a survey (US and the Netherlands, N=1396) that uniquely tasked people with providing hyperlinks to both misinformation and credible information, we explored the perceived span, availability, and accessibility of misinformation. Elevated levels across the three elements (span, availability, and accessibility) indicate heightened perceptions of misinformation nearness, where individuals experience misinformation as a threat that is highly accessible, universal, and intertwined in their everyday information ecology.

## Key Points

- Perceived misinformation nearness studied through span, availability, and accessibility dimensions
- Survey of 1396 respondents in US and Netherlands
- Participants provided hyperlinks to both misinformation and credible information
- Perception of misinformation grounded in subjective nearness, not objective exposure
- High perceived nearness makes misinformation feel universally accessible and threatening
