# What parents say about their teen's AI use | Pew Research Center

**Source:** [https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/what-parents-say-about-their-teens-ai-use/](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/what-parents-say-about-their-teens-ai-use/)

## Summary

This Pew Research Center report, published February 24, 2026, by researchers Colleen McClain, Monica Anderson, Olivia Sidoti, and William Bishop, explores how teenagers use and view artificial intelligence, and critically examines what parents observe and think about their teens' AI engagement. The study covers a wide range of topics including how teens utilize AI chatbots for schoolwork, their confidence levels in using AI tools, their awareness of AI's limitations and potential biases, and their overall attitudes toward the technology's role in society. A significant portion of the report is dedicated to parental perspectives, documenting what parents know about their teens' AI usage, the concerns they harbor, and the conversations happening within families about responsible AI use. The research also analyzes demographic differences in AI adoption patterns among teens, examining variations by age, gender, race, ethnicity, household income, and geographic location. Key findings reveal a complex picture: while many teens find AI tools genuinely useful for academic and creative tasks, parents express considerable unease about data privacy, the accuracy of AI-generated information, potential impacts on critical thinking skills, and the ethical implications of AI use. The report provides one of the most comprehensive contemporary data sets on the intersection of adolescence and artificial intelligence, offering valuable insights for educators, policymakers, and technology developers concerned with age-appropriate AI design and digital literacy education.

## Key Points

- Pew Research Center report on teens' AI use and parental perspectives
- Covers AI chatbots for schoolwork, demographic differences, parental concerns
- Complex picture: teens find AI useful but parents worry about privacy and critical thinking impact
