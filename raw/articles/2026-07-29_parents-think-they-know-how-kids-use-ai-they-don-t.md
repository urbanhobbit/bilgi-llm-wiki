# Parents think they know how kids use AI. They don't

**Source:** [https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20260317-how-kids-are-actually-using-ai](https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20260317-how-kids-are-actually-using-ai)

## Summary

This BBC Future article by Thomas Germain, published March 18, 2026, examines the striking disconnect between what parents believe about their children's AI usage and what teenagers actually do with AI tools in their daily lives. The article profiles 17-year-old New York City student Isis Joseph, who uses AI daily for homework assistance, restaurant recommendations, poetry inspiration and even personal advice on sensitive life issues. The piece draws on new survey data showing that while many parents believe AI use is limited to academic cheating or casual experimentation, teens are integrating AI into multiple dimensions of their lives including creative work, emotional support, personal decision-making, and self-directed learning outside formal educational settings. The article discusses how parents often perceive AI as a vast and threatening technology while simultaneously underestimating how deeply and routinely it has been woven into their children's daily digital experiences. Experts quoted in the piece suggest that this parent-teen knowledge gap around AI mirrors earlier gaps around social media use but is potentially more consequential given AI's unique ability to generate personalized, persuasive, and emotionally resonant content that can influence teen behavior and beliefs. The article also explores broader implications for digital literacy education policy and the pressing need for parents to engage more substantively and regularly with their children about AI's capabilities, limitations, and ethical dimensions.

## Key Points

- BBC article explores parent-teen AI knowledge gap
- Teens use AI for homework, emotional support, poetry, decision-making
- Parents underestimate breadth and depth of AI integration in teen lives
- Gap mirrors earlier social media knowledge gaps but more consequential
- Need for digital literacy education and substantive parent engagement
- AI's personalized content generation raises unique protection concerns
