# Shackle or hammer? How polarisation influences political corruption

**Author(s):** Áron Hajnal, Aslinur Inalci, Veronika Patkós

**Published:** 2026-06-23

**Source:** [https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21599165.2026.2686979](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21599165.2026.2686979)

## Summary

This article examines the complex relationship between political polarization and political corruption in the context of East European politics. The study investigates whether polarization acts as a 'shackle' (constraining corruption through increased scrutiny and accountability) or a 'hammer' (enabling corruption by creating polarized environments where oversight breaks down). The research contributes to understanding how democratic backsliding and affective polarization interact with governance quality in Central and Eastern Europe.

## Key Points

- Examines dual mechanisms of polarization on corruption: constraining vs enabling
- Context of East European politics and democratic backsliding
- Relates to affective polarization and institutional trust debates
- Contributes to literature on polarization's real-world governance consequences
