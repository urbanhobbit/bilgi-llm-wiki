# Youth Social Media Ban Passes State Legislature Over Privacy and Big Tech Concerns — The ACK Report

**Source:** [https://www.ack.net/education/reps-pass-youth-social-media-ban-over-privacy-big-tech-concerns/article5a0029f0-0ed8-4730-8222-017ca1f1c10e.html](https://www.ack.net/education/reps-pass-youth-social-media-ban-over-privacy-big-tech-concerns/article5a0029f0-0ed8-4730-8222-017ca1f1c10e.html)

## Summary

The ACK reports that state representatives have passed a comprehensive youth social media ban, citing serious and growing concerns about privacy violations by major technology companies, the exploitation of young users' personal data for commercial purposes, and the documented harmful impacts of social media on adolescent mental health and development. The legislation, which passed with bipartisan support, addresses multiple dimensions of the child online safety challenge including mandatory age verification requirements for social media platforms, parental consent provisions for users under 18, restrictions on targeted advertising and behavioral profiling of minors, requirements for transparent algorithm disclosure, and enhanced data protection obligations for platforms popular with young users. The bill includes enforcement mechanisms with meaningful penalties for non-compliant companies and establishes a state-level task force to monitor implementation and effectiveness. The legislative debate highlighted the tension between protecting children from online harms and preserving free expression rights, with careful attention to ensuring the legislation is constitutionally sound and proportionately targeted. The bill's passage reflects growing frustration among lawmakers with the pace of voluntary industry action and increasing public demand for stronger government regulation of children's digital experiences.

## Key Points

- State representatives pass youth social media ban citing privacy and mental health
- Age verification and parental consent requirements included
- Restrictions on targeted advertising to minors part of legislation
