# Large-N Qualitative Analysis (LNQA): Causal Generalization in Case Study and Multimethod Research

**Author(s):** Gary Goertz, Stephan Haggard

**Published:** 2023-08-23

**Source:** [https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/largen-qualitative-analysis-lnqa-causal-generalization-in-case-study-and-multimethod-research/7A848B3BCC7E01924F32754B7ACCE5B3](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/largen-qualitative-analysis-lnqa-causal-generalization-in-case-study-and-multimethod-research/7A848B3BCC7E01924F32754B7ACCE5B3)

## Summary

This article describes an emerging research practice called Large-N Qualitative Analysis (LNQA), outlining its core components and codifying best practice. LNQA starts with hypothesized regularities and causal mechanisms. Regularities take two basic forms: Y generalizations (if Y then X) or X generalizations (if X then Y). To establish a causal generalization requires defining its scope. The strength of the regularity is simply the percentage of cases conforming with the causal claim. The causal force of LNQA comes from within-case causal inference, which demonstrates the presence and operation of the postulated mechanisms in all cases in the scope. The method thus partly obviates problems arising from case selection in qualitative and multimethod work. The authors also identify a multimethod variant (M-LNQA) which combines LNQA with experimental, quasi-experimental, or observational statistical analysis. The appendix introduces over fifty examples of the method in practice.

## Key Points

- LNQA bridges large-N and qualitative traditions for causal generalization
- Two types of generalizations: Y generalizations and X generalizations
- Within-case causal inference is the core of LNQA's causal force
- M-LNQA variant combines LNQA with statistical analysis methods
- Over 50 examples of the method in practice appendix
