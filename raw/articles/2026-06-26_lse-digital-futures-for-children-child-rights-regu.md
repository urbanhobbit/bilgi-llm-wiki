# LSE Digital Futures for Children: Child Rights Regulation - Academic Research Event

**Source:** [https://www.lse.ac.uk/digital-futures-for-children/events/child-rights-regulation/home.aspx](https://www.lse.ac.uk/digital-futures-for-children/events/child-rights-regulation/home.aspx)

## Summary

The London School of Economics and Political Science (LSE) Digital Futures for Children research centre's event on child rights regulation represents an important academic forum for interdisciplinary research on children's digital rights. The research centre is a leading academic institution in children's digital rights research, employing interdisciplinary methodologies spanning law, sociology, psychology, media studies, and computer science. The event brings together academic researchers, policymakers, child rights advocates, and technology industry representatives to examine the evolving regulatory landscape for children's digital rights, including the UNCRC General Comment on children's rights in the digital environment, the EU Digital Services Act's child protection provisions, and national regulatory frameworks. Methodologically, the event features presentations employing doctrinal legal analysis, comparative law methodology, empirical research on children's digital experiences, and policy evaluation frameworks. The research centre's academic work is characterized by methodological pluralism, combining traditional legal and social science methods with participatory research approaches that center children's voices and experiences. The event also addresses methodological challenges in digital child rights research including measuring regulatory effectiveness and developing indicators for children's digital well-being. The interdisciplinary approach of the Digital Futures for Children research centre exemplifies how academic research can effectively bridge scholarly inquiry and policy impact. The participatory research methods that center children's voices provide an important methodological corrective to adult-centric research on children's digital experiences. The event format integrating researchers, policymakers, and advocates demonstrates a knowledge exchange model that enhances research impact. For Turkish research centres and academic institutions studying children's digital rights, this LSE model offers a replicable framework for organizing interdisciplinary, impact-oriented research programmes. The attention to the UNCRC General Comment provides an international human rights framework that can ground domestic research and policy analysis in universally recognized principles. The methodological challenges identified in measuring regulatory effectiveness point to important areas for methodological development in Turkish academic research on digital regulation.

## Key Points

- LSE Digital Futures for Children employs interdisciplinary research methodology spanning law, sociology, and computer science
- Participatory research approaches center children's voices and experiences in rights-focused research
- Event examines UNCRC General Comment, EU DSA, and national regulatory frameworks for children's digital rights
- Methodological challenges include measuring regulatory effectiveness and developing digital well-being indicators
- Research centre's methodological pluralism influences UK and international child rights policy development
